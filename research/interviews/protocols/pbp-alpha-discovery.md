# PBP Alpha Discovery Interview Protocol

Status: first-wave protocol
Owner: Product research
Last updated: 2026-05-10

## Purpose

Use this protocol for early discovery interviews with play-by-post writers,
directors, staff moderators, applicants, and returning regulars. The goal is
to understand current PBP workflows, identity practices, safety expectations,
discovery behavior, director burden, and modern product expectations before
turning Elbysodic assumptions into product doctrine.

This is not a sales call, usability test, or validation script. Treat current
personas as hypotheses. Interviewees may match several roles, reject the role
labels, or describe a culture that Elbysodic should learn from without copying.

## Who To Interview

Recruit a broad first wave across active and lapsed PBP cultures:

- Active scene writers with current `needs reply`, `waiting`, `caught up`, and
  watched-thread habits.
- Directors, admins, or staff moderators who manage rosters, claims, reserves,
  applications, wanted ads, events, rules, safety, and continuity.
- New or invited applicants who recently evaluated a community, submitted a
  first face, reserved a wanted, or bounced before joining.
- Hook hunters who browse wanted ads, plotters, relationship requests, open
  scenes, and event openings.
- Safety-boundary writers who care about content warnings, privacy, consent,
  staff visibility, off-ramps, and pseudonymous separation.
- Returning regulars who left PBP or moved to Discord, Tumblr, Reddit, RPR,
  RPoL, Google Docs, Notion, or private hybrid stacks.

Avoid over-indexing on one board genre, age cohort, platform, or staff culture.
Record the participant's self-described role and ecosystem instead of forcing a
persona label.

## Consent Script

Read or paraphrase before recording notes:

> Thanks for talking with me. I am researching how people run, join, and write
> in play-by-post communities. This is discovery research, not a test of you or
> your community.
>
> I will take notes. With your permission, I may record only to make sure the
> notes are accurate. Recordings will not be published. You can decline
> recording, skip any question, pause, or stop at any time.
>
> Please avoid sharing private names, Discord handles, private logs, exact board
> URLs, or sensitive details unless you are comfortable with them being used in
> private research notes. We can keep examples anonymous.
>
> I will mark your consent level as one of: private only, unattributed quote,
> attributed quote, or publishable. Which level are you comfortable with?
>
> Nothing you say is a product commitment. We are looking for patterns,
> surprises, and open questions.

Confirm:

- Consent level: private only / unattributed / attributed / publishable.
- Recording consent: yes / no.
- Follow-up consent: yes / no, and preferred public-safe channel if already
  cleared by the participant.

Do not commit private contact details, handles, raw recordings, or sensitive
participant data to the repository.

## Interviewer Guidance

- Ask for recent concrete examples before opinions: "Tell me about the last
  time..." beats "Would you use..."
- Preserve the participant's vocabulary. Note when they say face, muse, account,
  character, writer, member, player, admin, director, staff, or mod.
- Separate observed behavior from interpretation. Do not pitch Elbysodic as the
  answer to every pain point.
- Probe for tradeoffs: where they want structure, where they want flexibility,
  and where tooling would feel controlling.
- Watch for privacy boundaries around pseudonyms, staff notes, applications,
  safety topics, OOC history, and cross-community identity.
- Treat personas as working hypotheses. Tag them only after the interview and
  include evidence for the tag.
- Do not ask for live access to private boards, Discord servers, DMs, staff
  areas, or private applications.
- If a participant names harm, harassment, coercion, stalking, doxxing, or
  abuse, do not investigate details. Ask what product boundaries would help and
  offer to move on.

## 45-Minute Structure

1. Opening and consent - 5 minutes
   - Confirm consent level, recording, role, current ecosystem, and whether
     examples should be anonymized.

2. Recent PBP context - 5 minutes
   - Ask what they currently write, run, browse, or recently left. Capture
     platform mix and community size in broad terms.

3. Workflow walkthrough - 10 minutes
   - Have them narrate a recent session: entering a community, choosing a face,
     checking replies, reading context, writing, posting, plotting, or handling
     staff work.

4. Pain, safety, and identity - 10 minutes
   - Probe wrong-face risks, missed replies, fragmented context, boundaries,
     staff visibility, privacy, and trust.

5. Discovery and director burden - 7 minutes
   - Ask how people find hooks, judge fit, onboard, manage claims/reserves,
     keep rosters current, and preserve continuity.

6. Concept test - 5 minutes
   - Briefly describe Elbysodic as a roleplay-native studio layer for PBP
     communities. Test reactions to the concept, not screens or feature copy.

7. Wrap and follow-up - 3 minutes
   - Ask what was missed, confirm follow-up permission, and record any
     referrals or communities to learn from without capturing private contact
     data in repo notes.

## Question Bank

Use 8-12 questions total. Prioritize recent examples and follow the
participant's lane.

### Workflow

- Walk me through the last time you checked what you owed in a PBP community.
- How do you know which scenes need your reply, which are waiting, and which
  are caught up?
- What happens between reading the latest beat and posting your next reply?
- How do you choose or confirm the face you are posting as?
- Where do drafts, previews, notes, and OOC planning live today?
- What parts of a thread, scene, roster, claim, reserve, wanted, or plotter
  flow are easiest to lose track of?

### Pain

- Tell me about a recent moment where the tools got in the way of writing.
- When have you missed a reply, lost context, lost a draft, or posted in the
  wrong place?
- What workarounds does your community rely on that everyone accepts but nobody
  loves?
- What makes a board feel abandoned, brittle, or hard to trust?
- What do you tolerate because "that is just how PBP works"?

### Identity

- How do you separate writer identity, member identity, face, character, and
  staff role?
- What should happen when one writer owns multiple faces?
- Where does identity switching need to be fast, explicit, or hard to do by
  accident?
- What information belongs to the writer, the membership, the face, the
  character, or staff-only context?
- What would make you anxious about account, face, or character visibility?

### Safety

- How does your community communicate content boundaries, consent, warnings,
  and off-ramps?
- What should staff see, what should other writers see, and what should stay
  private?
- How are uncomfortable plotting requests, ghosting, conflict, or exits handled
  today?
- Where do current tools make pseudonymous safety easier or harder?
- What safety affordances would feel supportive, and what would feel like
  surveillance or overreach?

### Discovery

- How do you decide whether a community is alive, safe, compatible, and worth
  joining?
- How do you browse wanted hooks, open scenes, plotters, claims, reserves, or
  roster gaps?
- What signals help you judge cadence, post length, tone, boundaries, and OOC
  expectations before raising a hand?
- Tell me about the last hook or wanted that made you want to write.
- What makes discovery feel exciting versus like homework?

### Director Burden

- What recurring board-running work takes the most time?
- How do staff manage applications, revisions, claims, reserves, face lists,
  events, canons, rosters, and activity checks?
- Where does continuity live, and how do new or returning writers catch up?
- What director-authored material has to be structured instead of trapped in a
  thread?
- What staff controls need to exist without overwhelming ordinary writing
  surfaces?

### Modern Expectations

- Which non-forum tools have changed what you expect from PBP?
- What does "modern enough to trust" look like for a writing community?
- Where should PBP stay slow, durable, and thread-based?
- Where do fast notifications, mobile reading, Discord-style coordination, or
  richer media help?
- What would make a new PBP product feel like generic forum software in a new
  skin?

### Concept Test

Introduce only this lightweight concept:

"We are exploring Elbysodic as a roleplay-native studio layer for PBP
communities: one community per install for the MVP, tenant-aware by design,
with explicit membership identity, public faces/characters, durable scenes,
rosters, wanted hooks, claims, reserves, and director-authored board-running
material."

Then ask:

- What part sounds immediately useful, confusing, risky, or unnecessary?
- Which current workflow would you try first in that kind of tool?
- What would have to be true before your community trusted it?
- What would make it fail your culture even if the features were polished?
- Which words in that description feel right or wrong for your RP culture?

## What Not To Show

Do not show product screens, prototypes, feature lists, pricing, roadmap dates,
architecture diagrams, permission matrices, database models, AI features, or
internal persona documents in first-wave discovery. Do not compare against named
competitors in a way that leads the participant. Do not ask participants to
evaluate specific UI copy unless the interview has been explicitly converted
into a later usability or concept-copy session.

## Follow-Up Handling

- Record follow-up permission and topic, not private contact details, in repo
  notes.
- If a participant offers screenshots, exports, private board access, logs, DMs,
  or staff materials, decline by default. If a later research need requires
  private material, get explicit written permission and store it outside the
  repository.
- Send follow-up only through channels the participant already approved.
- When asking for referrals, request public community names, public links, or a
  participant-mediated introduction. Do not ask for private handles to be stored
  in repo notes.
- If a finding appears stable across interviews, promote it into synthesis with
  source IDs and uncertainty. Product implications remain proposed until
  accepted in `docs/`, `plans/`, or steward guidance.
- If an interview contradicts a persona, preserve the contradiction as a
  minority report rather than smoothing it into the existing hypothesis.
