# Elbysodic Concept Brief

Status: interview artifact
Date: 2026-05-10
Audience: PBP writers, directors, staff moderators, theme maintainers, and
adjacent online roleplay operators
Use: Share this brief for concept critique after discovery context is
understood, or for async reviewers who need an artifact. Do not use it as a
replacement for problem discovery.

## One-Sentence Concept

Elbysodic is a PBP-native source-of-truth studio: a durable forum backbone with
first-class tools for faces, scenes, wanted hooks, applications, claims,
reserves, Studio workflows, and community appearance.

## What It Is

Elbysodic starts from the assumption that play-by-post communities are not
generic forums and not disposable chat servers. They are living writing spaces
where character identity, thread continuity, community aesthetic control, and
pseudonymous safety matter.

The product keeps the forum as the backbone: boards, threads, scenes, rosters,
guidebooks, archives, and long-form replies remain durable and readable. Around
that backbone, Elbysodic adds a studio layer for the board-running work that PBP
communities already do: managing faces, wanted hooks, applications, claims,
reserves, casting, scene state, continuity, staff review, and appearance.

The goal is not to flatten roleplay cultures into one generic social app. The
goal is to make common PBP primitives explicit enough that directors and
writers are not forced to maintain the real source of truth in scattered docs,
Discord channels, spreadsheets, skin hacks, and memory.

## Core Promise

- Writers can understand who is active, who needs replies, who is waiting, who
  is caught up, and which scenes are watching without losing long-form flow.
- Characters remain public posting identities owned inside one community, not
  global profiles.
- Membership carries community identity, roles, permissions, names, and
  defaults; characters carry public authorship and story context.
- Directors can manage applications, claims, reserves, wanted hooks, rosters,
  scenes, and continuity as native workflows instead of forcing everything into
  threads.
- Communities keep atmosphere and appearance control through safe, constrained
  tools rather than raw CSS, scripts, or layout-breaking theme inputs.
- The product earns trust through clear boundaries before adding faster or
  more automated surfaces.

## First-Class Areas

Faces:

Face identity matters in PBP. Elbysodic treats faces as explicit community
objects connected to characters, applications, claims, reserves, availability,
and display defaults.

Scenes and threads:

Scenes and threads remain durable writing records. Scene state should make it
easy to see needs reply, waiting, caught up, active, archived, and watching
without breaking the writing experience.

Wanted hooks:

Wanted ads are not generic classifieds. They are casting and plot handoffs tied
to community context, relationship needs, face preferences, applications, and
claim/reserve workflows.

Applications:

Applications should support staff review, applicant clarity, character setup,
face handling, and community-specific expectations without turning every
community into the same template.

Claims and reserves:

Claims and reserves need explicit state, expiry, staff visibility, and conflict
handling. They should not live only in freeform posts that staff must manually
audit.

Studio:

Studio is the staff/director workbench for board-running material: rosters,
applications, claims, reserves, wanted hooks, scenes, continuity, guidebook
material, and operational review.

Appearance:

Appearance should let a community feel like itself while protecting
readability, safety, accessibility, and layout stability. The target is curated
control, not unlimited raw theming.

## Later, Not First

Rapid-touch interaction:

Quick IC texts, chatbox-style banter, phone-call transcripts, and lightweight
coordination may belong in Elbysodic later. They should support the durable
record rather than replace scenes, threads, rosters, or Studio workflows.

AI:

AI is intentionally parked for now. Early research should focus on identity,
continuity, staff workflow, writing flow, appearance, privacy, and trust. AI
should not be used to replace human replies, generate roleplay by default, or
become the product's headline before the core PBP source-of-truth layer earns
confidence.

## What This Is Not

- Not a generic forum skin.
- Not a Discord replacement.
- Not an AI roleplay bot.
- Not a freeform website builder.
- Not a global character network where faces or characters float across
  communities by default.
- Not a tool that gives user-level staff power detached from community
  membership.

## What We Need To Learn

After reading this brief, please critique the concept as a PBP participant or
operator. The most useful feedback is concrete, skeptical, and culturally
specific.

Research questions:

1. Which parts feel native to PBP, and which parts sound like generic forum or
   SaaS language?
2. What would make you distrust this as a writer, applicant, director, staff
   moderator, or theme maintainer?
3. Which workflows must exist before you would believe the source-of-truth
   promise: faces, scenes, wanted, applications, claims, reserves, Studio,
   appearance, or something else?
4. What should stay in durable forum structures, and what can safely become a
   faster rapid-touch adjunct later?
5. What community appearance controls are essential, and which controls would
   create safety, accessibility, privacy, or maintenance problems?
6. How should Elbysodic handle applicants, new faces, hook hunters, returning
   regulars, and staff without exposing private or staff-only information?
7. What would make repo access useful for your critique, and what should be
   proven with a guided demo or written concept first?
8. What would you explicitly not want Elbysodic to automate, especially around
   writing, replies, applications, moderation, or AI?
9. What modern online roleplay habits should Elbysodic learn from without
   copying directly?
10. What is one failure mode that would make this feel unsafe, culturally wrong,
    or not worth adopting?
