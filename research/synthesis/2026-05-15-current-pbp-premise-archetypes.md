## Claim

Current public Jcink/PBP advertising is less genre-first than premise-engine
first. The strongest recurring offers are not just "real life," "fantasy," or
"supernatural." They are repeatable play promises: a small-town social web, a
weird-town mystery, a supernatural city under pressure, a high-fantasy faction
crisis, a canon-divergent AU, a fame/industry pressure cooker, structured
survival/trials, historical pressure, or a strange frontier.

For Elbysodic, the useful taxonomy should classify communities by the kind of
thread and plotting loop they create, then layer genre, rating, activity,
application, and access metadata on top.

## Scope And Caveats

This synthesis uses public ads and directory pages as source signal. It does
not claim to measure the whole PBP market. Direct crawling of the user's Avior
forum link was blocked by robots in the browser, so Avior is represented
through its All Things Roleplay ad mirror.

The sample is weighted toward English-language Jcink boards that advertise on
Tumblr, RP ad hubs, and Discord listing pages. It undercounts private Discord
groups, Dreamwidth communities, Reddit 1x1 partner searches, tabletop PbP, and
communities that rely on word of mouth.

## Premise Archetype System

Use one primary archetype per community, with secondary tags for tone, access,
and operating model. The primary archetype should answer: "What kind of scene
does this place make easy to start and sustain?"

Recommended dimensions:

| Dimension | Values to expose |
| --- | --- |
| Premise archetype | Small-town social web, weird-town mystery, urban supernatural, high-fantasy crisis, canon-divergent AU, fame/industry drama, survival/trials, historical pressure, sci-fi frontier |
| Genre tags | Real life, slice of life, supernatural, horror, fantasy, historical, sci-fi, fandom-inspired, celeb, animal, western, dystopian |
| Play engine | Character-driven, plot-driven, event-driven, mystery-driven, faction-driven, canon-driven, institution-driven |
| Lore aperture | Closed canon, canon-divergent, semi-open lore, open lore, original lore, low-lore real life |
| Access model | Public, semi-private, invite/referral, interest form, member app, character app, profile app, short app |
| Rating and age | 18+, 21+, 25+, 3/3/3 or L/S/V style rating, content notes |
| Pace and labor | No word count, relaxed activity, biweekly/monthly checks, light activity, plot events, no activity checks |
| Forum plus adjunct | Discord for plotting, Discord for onboarding, Discord for lore drops, minimal Discord, forum-first |
| Connection scaffolds | Wanted ads, face claims, reserves, canons, families, shippers, trackers, requests, event prompts |

## Archetypes

### 1. Small-Town Social Web

Core promise: bring a face into a named town where family, work, romance,
gossip, returning-home arcs, seasonal events, and cross-plotting generate
threads.

Source signal:
- View Between Villages, Another Slow Burn, Sugar High, All Roads Lead Home,
  Among the Wildflowers, Under Pressure, H63, and similar ads foreground
  fictional towns or real cities with profile apps, wanted ads, face claims,
  relaxed activity, no word count, community warmth, and family plots.
- The premise usually does not need a central metaplot. It needs a dense roster
  of relationships, businesses, neighborhoods, events, and open connections.

Writer jobs:
- "I want a low-friction character home."
- "I want durable relationships and messy social threads."
- "I need wanted ads and family ties so my first face does not start alone."
- "I want activity expectations that respect adult life."

Explore needs:
- Town vibe, age/rating, activity pressure, app type, wanted count, open face
  claims, open family/coworker hooks, active events, Discord posture, recent
  threads.

Elbysodic implication: this should be one of the first curated communities.
It exercises rosters, faces, claims, wanted hooks, local events, needs reply,
waiting, caught up, and member warmth without heavy lore tooling.

Confidence: high within the sampled Jcink ad stream.

### 2. Weird-Town Mystery

Core promise: a cozy or ordinary town with something wrong beneath it. Writers
get slice-of-life entry points plus prompts, encounters, secrets, disappearances,
cryptids, ghosts, cults, or thin-veil phenomena.

Source signal:
- Avior uses open-lore supernatural species, an observatory, researchers,
  haunted locations, prompts, and encounters.
- Living Restless, Lost in Lethe, Midnight at Hemlock Bay, The Grass is
  Greener, and Forgotten Legends use haunted/cozy-small-town imagery, rumors,
  disappearances, cryptids, town whispers, and player-driven change.

Writer jobs:
- "I want an approachable first thread, but I also want mystery stakes."
- "I want staff prompts that move the plot without railroading every scene."
- "I want a human, supernatural, hunter, researcher, skeptic, or local angle."

Explore needs:
- Open species rules, mystery maturity, staff prompt cadence, current chapter,
  encounter system, canon event timeline, public/private lore split, safety
  notes.

Elbysodic implication: this is the best second curated community after
small-town social web because it tests continuity graph needs early: prompts,
encounters, source-linked events, rumors, chapter progress, and canon review.

Confidence: high within the sampled Jcink ad stream.

### 3. Urban Supernatural Pressure Cooker

Core promise: a city where supernatural groups, governments, hunters, factions,
crime, celebrity, secrecy, prejudice, and political intrigue collide.

Source signal:
- Las Valencia, Bloody Echoes, Noctis Hortum, Novus Orbis, Dark Tranquility,
  and similar ads present vampires, werewolves, witches, shifters, fae, ghosts,
  hunters, anti-supernatural groups, laws, clutches, and faction pressure.
- These communities often advertise 3/3/3, mature themes, open or semi-open
  lore, no word count, relaxed activity, and a roster of species pages.

Writer jobs:
- "I want power dynamics and faction politics."
- "I want to write danger, romance, secrecy, and moral compromise."
- "I want a supernatural face with species lore but not a closed canon cage."

Explore needs:
- Species list, banned/limited concepts, power scaling, faction status, current
  subplot, mature content boundaries, staff approval burden, available
  wanted/canon roles.

Elbysodic implication: this archetype needs species/faction claims, power
boundaries, staff review queues, content warnings, and privacy-aware lore
visibility. It is attractive but heavier to seed well.

Confidence: high within the sampled Jcink ad stream.

### 4. High-Fantasy Crisis And Court Politics

Core promise: enter an original or loosely inspired fantasy world at a point of
war, depleted magic, succession crisis, demon conflict, faction collapse, or
court intrigue.

Source signal:
- Rifts, Wide Awake, Carry the Flame, Hollow Realm, Echoes of Sin and
  Whispers, and ACOTAR/Kushiel-like ads foreground magic systems, courts,
  councils, wars, rifts, relics, demons, sanctuary lands, political plots, and
  worldbuilding.
- Several use profile apps, no word count, 3/3/3 or 3/2/3 ratings, and
  Discord plus guidebook links.

Writer jobs:
- "I want lore depth, factions, stakes, and event arcs."
- "I want room for OCs inside a setting with strong aesthetic identity."
- "I want court/faction connections so my face immediately matters."

Explore needs:
- Lore size, faction openings, magic rules, power tiers, current chapter,
  wanted roles, guidebook complexity, application requirements, event calendar.

Elbysodic implication: this should be an initial curated community only if the
blueprint can ship with constrained lore, faction templates, and a clean first
face path. Otherwise it risks recreating the heavy guidebook/app burden that
Elbysodic wants to reduce.

Confidence: high within the sampled Jcink ad stream.

### 5. Canon-Divergent AU

Core promise: use a familiar IP or genre canon as shared shorthand, then branch
into an alternate timeline where canon characters, original characters, or both
can reshape events.

Source signal:
- Current ads include Dance of the Dragons era AU, Harry Potter/Marauders-era
  AUs, ACOTAR-inspired courts, Kushiel-inspired AU, Dragon Age AU, ASOIAF, TVD
  plus Teen Wolf plus Supernatural panfandom, and Jujutsu Kaisen/Arknights
  style Discord RP examples outside strict Jcink.
- The ad language repeatedly says AU, canon-divergent, loosely based, canon
  adjacent, canons, character list, reserves, and early plotting.

Writer jobs:
- "I want shared canon fluency without replaying canon."
- "I want desirable canons, original characters, or canon-adjacent roles."
- "I want an AU where my character can matter."

Explore needs:
- Canon policy, OC policy, canon availability, timeline branch point,
  spoiler/version assumptions, face/canon reserves, whether knowledge of canon
  is required.

Elbysodic implication: Elbysodic should learn from this demand but avoid
launching official seeded IP communities. Offer "canon-inspired grammar"
through original templates: noble houses, academy war, court politics, monster
town, chosen factions, legacy rebellion, and fandom-adjacent vibe tags without
hosting infringing starter realms.

Confidence: high that demand exists; low that it should be an Elbysodic-owned
seed category in literal IP form.

### 6. Fame, Industry, And Surveillance Drama

Core promise: write public/private identity under pressure: celebrities,
artists, musicians, entertainment workers, gossip, privacy loss, AI anxiety,
public image, scandals, money, and ambition.

Source signal:
- Pretty Venom, Almost Famous, Eyez Everywhere, and Confess My Truth ads point
  to real-life/celeb, entertainment-industry drama, "fame comes with a price,"
  gossip, psychological horror, surveillance, AI impact, and desired wanted
  connections.

Writer jobs:
- "I want glamorous mess, public image, private relationships, and career
  arcs."
- "I want modern themes without full supernatural lore."
- "I want wanted links so my face enters an existing network."

Explore needs:
- Celebrity policy, fictional vs real-person boundaries, privacy rules,
  gossip/news mechanics, career categories, public/private scene boundaries,
  wanted relationships.

Elbysodic implication: this is promising as a curated initial community because
it exercises public/private identity, face claims, rumors, event calendars, and
media objects. It needs explicit rules around real-person play, consent, and
harassment posture.

Confidence: medium. The archetype is visible but less frequent than small-town,
supernatural, and fantasy in the sampled stream.

### Excluded Pattern: Multi-Genre Sanctuary Sandbox

Core promise: a low-restriction place for writers tired of drama, toxicity, or
being told what they cannot write. The offer is community safety plus broad
creative permission rather than a sharply authored world.

Source signal:
- Sanctuary, Blue Hour, and resource/search hubs advertise multi-genre or
  sandbox play, freedom to write, little restriction, low drama, inclusivity,
  and large active communities.

Writer jobs:
- "I want somewhere to write without fitting a narrow premise."
- "I want to bring many kinds of faces and plots."
- "I want autonomy and low staff friction."

Explore needs:
- Genre boundaries, safety rules, how discovery works without a shared premise,
  whether continuity is per-thread/per-face/per-realm, how wanted ads and
  search are organized.

Elbysodic implication: do not seed this as a curated initial community. It is
source signal about writer frustration and safety needs, but it is not a
premise-based community. Its useful parts should be absorbed into clearer
premises as safety posture, compatibility metadata, and flexible plotting.

Confidence: medium.

### 8. Survival, Trials, And Structured Competition

Core promise: characters belong to clans, factions, districts, packs, schools,
or teams under scarcity, games, trials, ranks, dice, tasks, or survival
pressure.

Source signal:
- Messaires advertises canine clans, famine aftermath, The Order, seasonal
  Trials, deadly challenges, and resistance.
- PNW Clans and Warrior Cats style ads show animal clan identity, territories,
  gatherings, and Discord-for-plotting/forum-for-writing.
- Some anime/game Discord RP examples use stats, dice, routes, moderation, and
  reward loops.

Writer jobs:
- "I want structured stakes, ranks, and group identity."
- "I want task prompts that create action scenes."
- "I want my face's obligations to a clan/faction to drive threads."

Explore needs:
- Species/faction type, rank availability, mortality policy, dice/task rules,
  staff moderation load, event cadence, character limits.

Elbysodic implication: powerful for future Program Blueprints, but not the
first curated slate unless the system supports event tasks, ranks, and fair
moderation tools cleanly.

Confidence: medium.

### 9. Historical Pressure

Core promise: write within a historical setting where social constraints,
class, empire, war, crime, etiquette, and changing institutions create drama.

Source signal:
- Absinthe presents 1880s London with working poverty, crime, industrialization,
  social reform, and upper-class gilding.
- Harry Potter 1950s, Marauders-era AUs, Kushiel medieval AU, ASOIAF, and
  Dragon Age AU blend historical texture with canon or fantasy.

Writer jobs:
- "I want social constraint and period atmosphere."
- "I want class, institution, reform, secrecy, or court ritual to matter."
- "I want historical texture but inclusive modern community standards."

Explore needs:
- Historical accuracy posture, inclusive adaptation policy, rating, content
  warnings, canon vs original history, guidebook burden, face/costume tone.

Elbysodic implication: historical pressure is a strong theme layer that can sit
on top of other archetypes. A pure historical starter realm is less obviously
central than small-town, supernatural, or fantasy, but period constraints can
differentiate a curated slate.

Confidence: medium.

### 10. Soft Sci-Fi, Alien, And Weird Frontier

Core promise: memory, abductions, desert towns, future cities, western edges,
or alien contact create mystery and identity tension without hard-science
simulation.

Source signal:
- Cullaways advertises soft sci-fi, aliens, Nevada, and memory.
- Rpings featured links include a sci-fi western premise.
- Las Valencia's 2082 supernatural city blurs future-city, horror, and fantasy.

Writer jobs:
- "I want the strangeness of sci-fi without a technical manual."
- "I want memory, identity, surveillance, aliens, or frontier isolation."
- "I want a stranger premise than another coastal town."

Explore needs:
- Technology level, alien/contact policy, memory/identity mechanics, safety
  boundaries, lore density, whether play is mystery, survival, romance, or
  sandbox.

Elbysodic implication: good as a distinctive third or fourth seed if positioned
as "strange frontier" rather than hard sci-fi. It can show that Elbysodic is
not only cozy towns and supernatural cities.

Confidence: low to medium in the sampled stream.

### Supporting Pattern: Wanted Hooks

Core signal: wanted ads are a major activation mechanism inside premise-based
communities. They are not a community premise by themselves.

Source signal:
- Current All Things Roleplay entries include specific wanted connections such
  as estranged family on a dystopian abilities site, art-student ritual fallout,
  second-chance magical realism romance, arranged marriage family needs, and
  canon families.
- Small-town and celebrity ads repeatedly steer newcomers toward wanted ads so
  the first face has immediate ties.

Writer jobs:
- "I do not know what to bring first."
- "I want a guaranteed first plot."
- "I want someone already waiting for my face."

Explore needs:
- Wanted hooks searchable by relationship type, intensity, genre, age range,
  face flexibility, writer availability, required lore knowledge, and whether
  the hook is still open.

Elbysodic implication: wanted hooks should be seeded inside every premise
community where they fit, but not treated as their own archetype, homepage
lane, or standalone seed community.

Confidence: high.

## Cross-Cutting Pattern Notes

- Age and rating are front-door metadata, not fine print. 18+, 21+, 25+, and
  3/3/3 appear repeatedly.
- Profile apps dominate the sampled Jcink ads. Short apps, member apps,
  interest forms, and referral/semi-private routes are visible alternatives.
- "No word count" and relaxed activity rules are repeated selling points.
  Adult writers are asking for seriousness of community without brittle output
  quotas.
- Discord is usually treated as an adjunct, not a replacement, for Jcink
  boards in the forum-first sample. It handles chatting, plotting, reserves,
  lore drops, onboarding, and community support.
- Accessibility and mobile polish are ad-worthy. Avior explicitly advertises
  contrast-tested colors, dark/light modes, responsiveness, and UserWay.
- Ads increasingly promise automation, code banks, trackers, shippers, mobile
  skins, profile systems, and app review speed. The product pain is not only
  premise discovery; it is operational drag.
- "Inclusive" is table stakes in the public ad language, but it needs product
  substance: content boundaries, reporting, privacy, consent, role clarity, and
  staff workflow.

## Curated Initial Community Slate

Recommended first slate:

1. Coastal town social web
   - Archetype: small-town social web.
   - Why: highest visible frequency, low lore burden, strong wanted/claims/
     roster fit, clear writer onboarding.
   - Must include: wanted hooks, family/workplace ties, seasonal events,
     face claims, no-word-count option, relaxed activity, thread status.

2. Open-lore weird town
   - Archetype: weird-town mystery.
   - Why: matches Avior-style signal and combines approachable real-life
     threads with prompts, encounters, and continuity.
   - Must include: open species/ability submission, prompt/encounter engine,
     chapter timeline, rumor board, staff canon review.

3. Supernatural city
   - Archetype: urban supernatural pressure cooker.
   - Why: high-density source signal and clear premise difference from
     weird-town mystery.
   - Must include: species rules, faction claims, power limits, hunters or
     legal pressure, nightlife/businesses, and mature content boundaries.

4. Original court-and-faction fantasy
   - Archetype: high-fantasy crisis without literal IP dependency.
   - Why: captures fantasy AU/court politics demand while avoiding official
     seed communities based on protected IP.
   - Must include: faction openings, concise magic constraints, event calendar,
     wanted roles, lore primer, claims for ranks/titles.

5. Original canon-adjacent AU
   - Archetype: canon-divergent AU without literal IP dependency.
   - Why: captures demand for canons, branch points, and shared shorthand
     while keeping Elbysodic-owned seeds original.
   - Must include: original canon roster, branch-point timeline, reserves,
     open OCs, faction or house openings, and "inspired-by not IP" posture.

6. Spotlight city
   - Archetype: fame, industry, and surveillance drama.
   - Why: differentiates the slate and tests public/private identity, rumors,
     media objects, and gossip mechanics.
   - Must include: fictional celebrity policy, public/private scene labels,
     rumor/news workflow, career categories, consent guardrails.

7. Trial clans or academy
   - Archetype: survival/trials and structured competition.
   - Why: covers rank, obligation, tasks, teams, and event-driven play.
   - Must include: ranks, teams/clans/classes, task prompts, fair
     adjudication, event cadence, and consequence policy.

8. Occult historical city
   - Archetype: historical pressure.
   - Why: captures period constraint, class/institution drama, occult texture,
     and social pressure without leaning on literal fandom.
   - Must include: period primer, inclusive adaptation policy, content
     warnings, institutions, neighborhoods, and staff guidance.

9. Strange frontier
   - Archetype: soft sci-fi/weird frontier.
   - Why: broader genre coverage and a more distinctive discovery hook than
     another town or supernatural city.
   - Must include: memory/contact rules, tech level, isolation map, limited
     factions, strong safety notes.

Not recommended as an official first seed:

- Literal IP/canon boards. The demand is real, but Elbysodic should provide
  original structures and inspiration tags rather than platform-owned fandom
  communities.
- Multi-genre sanctuary sandbox as the flagship. It has demand, but it weakens
  product clarity and relies heavily on discovery/search quality before a
  shared premise can carry onboarding.
- Wanted-hook-first communities. Wanted hooks are valuable connective tissue
  inside a premise, but they should not replace the premise.
- Mechanics-heavy survival/trials as the first implementation target. The
  premise is valid, but it needs ranks, tasks, dice or adjudication,
  consequence policy, and fairness tooling.

## Product Implications

Accepted:
- Exploration should classify by premise archetype plus writer job, not only
  broad genre.
- Wanted hooks should be a first-class supporting object inside premise
  communities, not a standalone archetype.
- Age/rating, activity pressure, application type, and Discord posture belong
  on cards and filters.
- Initial communities should be original, not official fandom/IP seeds.

Proposed:
- Add "premise archetype" and "play engine" to Program Blueprint metadata.
- Add "lore aperture" as a reusable taxonomy: closed canon, canon-divergent,
  semi-open, open lore, original lore, low-lore real life.
- Treat current event/chapter as a surfaced exploration field so live realms
  advertise movement, not just setting.
- Use wanted-hook metadata to enrich premise community cards and onboarding.

Deferred:
- Full multi-genre sandbox support until discovery, boundaries, and continuity
  can keep broad play legible.
- Survival/trials blueprint until task, dice, rank, and moderation tooling are
  available.
- Direct fandom/canon tooling decisions until legal/product boundaries are
  explicit.

Rejected:
- A genre-only home page such as real life/fantasy/supernatural/sci-fi as the
  primary taxonomy. It hides the actual writer job and makes many modern ads
  look more similar than they are.
- Treating Discord as the product center for Elbysodic's PBP strategy. The
  public Jcink signal repeatedly shows Discord as useful adjunct, while the
  durable story remains forum-first.
