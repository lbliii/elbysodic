# PBP Alpha Recruiting Plan

Status: proposed research plan
Date: 2026-05-10
Owner: Product research
Scope: Research outreach for early Elbysodic concept critique and alpha
learning. This is not a launch plan, sales plan, or public product commitment.
Confidence: medium

## Research Goal

Recruit a small, varied set of play-by-post and adjacent online roleplay
participants to critique Elbysodic as a PBP-native source-of-truth studio. The
goal is to learn where the concept feels culturally right, where it creates
distrust, what must remain forum-backed and durable, and which rapid-touch or AI
expectations should be parked until the core studio layer earns trust.

Use problem interviews first when the participant can speak from recent
experience. Use the one-page product concept brief for concept critique after
the problem context is understood, or for async reviewers who need a concrete
artifact. The repo should not be treated as an interview artifact until a
participant has clear context, consent, and a concrete reason to inspect it.

## Recruiting Principles

- Use public, respectful contact paths only.
- Ask for critique, not endorsement.
- Keep outreach short and specific to the recipient's public work or role.
- Do not collect private handles, emails, DMs, recordings, or sensitive
  participant details in the repository.
- Offer feedback modes: 30-minute call, async written critique, or annotated
  concept brief.
- Record consent level before notes are summarized: private, unattributed,
  attributed, or publishable.
- Separate participant evidence from researcher inference and product
  implications.

## Waves

### Wave 0: Internal Calibration

Target size: 2 to 3 trusted readers or collaborators.

Purpose:

- Check whether the concept brief is understandable without repo context.
- Remove language that sounds too SaaS, too generic-forum, or too AI-forward.
- Verify that screening questions do not pressure people to disclose private
  community details.

Exit criteria:

- Brief can be read in under five minutes.
- Readers can explain the core promise back as "forum backbone plus studio
  source of truth for faces, scenes, wanted, applications, claims, reserves,
  Studio, and appearance."
- Outreach message makes clear that AI is parked and rapid-touch is later.

### Wave 1: Known PBP Operators And Staff

Target size: 5 to 8 conversations.

Segments:

- Current or recent forum PBP directors.
- Staff moderators who handle applications, claims, reserves, rosters, wanted
  ads, and activity checks.
- Skin/theme maintainers who understand community aesthetic control.
- Writers who have run multiple faces across threads and scenes.

Learning focus:

- Whether the source-of-truth studio model maps to real board-running work.
- Which workflows must be first-class rather than thread workarounds.
- Where membership, character authorship, staff permission, and privacy
  boundaries feel most fragile.
- What would make directors distrust an alpha.

### Wave 2: Hybrid And Displaced PBP Users

Target size: 6 to 10 conversations.

Segments:

- Discord RP operators who still miss durable scene records.
- Tumblr or indie RP writers who care about face identity, IC/OOC boundaries,
  and aesthetics.
- Reddit partner-search users who struggle with discovery, continuity, or
  trust before a thread starts.
- TTRPG PbP organizers who coordinate asynchronous scenes and continuity.

Learning focus:

- Which modern habits are genuinely useful and which are migration symptoms.
- How much rapid-touch interaction is needed later without replacing durable
  scenes.
- Whether "forum backbone" reads as comforting, dated, or incomplete.
- How these users evaluate safety, pseudonymity, and community atmosphere.

### Wave 3: Skeptics And Edge Cases

Target size: 4 to 6 conversations.

Segments:

- Writers who left forum PBP because of staff overhead, clique dynamics, or
  application friction.
- Moderators who have handled harassment, ban evasion, plagiarism, or privacy
  leaks.
- Mobile-heavy writers who expect low-friction replies and notifications.
- Accessibility-conscious users who critique heavy skins and unreadable
  layouts.

Learning focus:

- What Elbysodic must refuse, constrain, or explain before alpha.
- Which appearance controls are necessary versus dangerous.
- How to preserve long-form writing flow without recreating brittle forum
  rituals.
- What safety or trust threshold must be met before repo access or alpha access
  is appropriate.

## Target Segments

Prioritize breadth across roleplay cultures without flattening them:

- Forum PBP directors and staff.
- Long-form writers with multiple faces and active threads.
- New face applicants who recently navigated applications, claims, reserves,
  or wanted hooks.
- Hook hunters who browse wanted ads and rosters before joining.
- Skin, theme, and code maintainers from forum communities.
- Discord RP operators who keep separate docs, spreadsheets, or channels as
  continuity records.
- Tumblr/indie RP writers who care deeply about face identity, tone, and
  presentation.
- TTRPG PbP organizers who manage asynchronous scenes and out-of-band
  coordination.
- Safety-boundary writers who need pseudonymous control and clear private/staff
  boundaries.

## Screening Criteria

Good fit:

- Has participated in online roleplay in the last 24 months, or currently
  maintains a PBP-adjacent community.
- Can speak concretely about at least one of: faces, scenes, applications,
  claims, reserves, wanted ads, rosters, plotters, staff workflows, skins,
  thread continuity, or rapid-touch side channels.
- Is willing to critique a concept brief without seeing the repository first.
- Understands that this is research, not a feature vote or guaranteed invite.
- Can choose a consent level for summarized notes.

Prioritize for early waves:

- People who have operated a community, moderated applications/claims, or
  maintained continuity tools.
- People who bridge cultures, such as forum-to-Discord, Tumblr-to-forum, or
  TTRPG-to-PBP participants.
- People likely to name distrust, safety, staff burden, or aesthetic failure
  modes clearly.

Deprioritize:

- People only seeking a general social writing app.
- People whose main interest is AI-generated roleplay, automated writing, or
  replacing human replies.
- People who want unrestricted theming, raw CSS/script control, or external
  font injection as a first requirement.
- People who primarily want real-time chat with no durable scene or thread
  record.

## Who Not To Contact Yet

Do not contact these groups until the concept, consent language, and alpha
boundaries are sharper:

- Private or locked communities where public contact would violate local norms.
- Minors or youth-oriented spaces.
- Communities centered on sensitive personal material unless a trusted adult
  operator initiates contact and safety handling is defined.
- People in active conflict with a community, staff team, or platform.
- High-profile community owners whose endorsement could distort early research
  or create implied launch commitments.
- Competitor platform maintainers unless the ask is explicitly framed as
  research critique, not extraction.
- Anyone whose only reachable channel is a private DM path that would require
  storing private handles or private conversation details in the repo.

## Sample Outreach Message

```text
Hi <name>,

I'm researching modern play-by-post workflows for Elbysodic, a PBP-native
source-of-truth studio with a forum backbone. The core idea is to make faces,
scenes, wanted hooks, applications, claims, reserves, Studio workflows, and
community appearance first-class instead of scattering them across threads,
spreadsheets, Discord channels, and skin hacks.

Your public work on <specific community/project/topic> stood out because
<specific reason>. Would you be open to a 30-minute critique or async written
feedback on a one-page concept brief?

This is research, not a launch pitch. I am especially looking for what feels
culturally right, what would make writers or staff distrust it, what a PBP
platform should refuse to make configurable, and when repo or alpha access
would actually be useful.

Feedback can stay private, unattributed, attributed, or publishable depending
on your preference.
```

## Interview Prep

For discovery-first sessions, send before the session:

- A short note about the research topic.
- Consent options for summarized notes.
- A reminder not to share private community material.

For concept-critique sessions, send before the session:

- The one-page Elbysodic concept brief.
- A short note that AI is intentionally parked for now.
- A short note that rapid-touch interaction is a later adjunct, not the MVP
  center.
- Consent options for summarized notes.

Ask participants not to share:

- Private staff drama.
- Private member details.
- Screenshots from locked areas.
- Names, handles, or contact details that are not already public and cleared.

## When Repo Access Is Appropriate

Do not share the repo for initial recruiting. The repo is appropriate only when
at least one condition is true:

- The participant is giving implementation-level critique on data boundaries,
  privacy, accessibility, security, or architecture.
- The participant is a trusted collaborator or prospective contributor who has
  accepted the product frame and needs concrete code context.
- A director or staff operator needs to inspect a local alpha to evaluate
  workflow realism after concept-level critique.
- The participant explicitly asks for repo context after consent and
  confidentiality expectations are clear.

Before granting repo access:

- Confirm what parts of the repo they need and why.
- Explain that research docs may contain unfinished signal, not doctrine.
- Avoid exposing private participant notes or contact details.
- Prefer a guided walkthrough, read-only access, or a stripped demo artifact
  before broad repository access.

## Output From Each Conversation

For each participant, produce a summarized interview note with:

- Participant segment.
- Consent level.
- Source signal.
- Strong signals.
- Researcher inference.
- Product implications marked proposed, accepted, deferred, or rejected.
- Follow-up questions.

Do not commit raw recordings, private DMs, private handles, or sensitive
participant details.
