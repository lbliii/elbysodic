# Roleplay Research And Outreach Leads

Status: product research lead list
Owner: Product and UX stewardship
Last updated: 2026-05-10

This document tracks public writers, bloggers, platform builders, community
operators, and resource hubs who have thought seriously about play-by-post,
forum RP, asynchronous TTRPG play, simming, Tumblr/Jcink roleplay, MMO RP, or
roleplay tooling.

Use this as a reading list and future alpha/beta outreach map. Do not treat
anyone listed here as endorsing Elbysodic. Do not scrape private contact
information. Use only public contact forms, public social links, asks, comments,
or community channels, and approach people as research collaborators, not sales
targets.

## Outreach Principles

- Lead with curiosity: "We are researching modern roleplay workflows and would
  value your critique" is better than a launch pitch.
- Ask for a short interview, written feedback, or alpha walkthrough. Do not ask
  them to migrate a community first.
- Be explicit about what Elbysodic is and is not: a roleplay-native studio and
  source-of-truth layer, not a Discord replacement or generic forum skin.
- Offer concrete prompts: what do they think PBP gets wrong, what should never
  be lost, what tools would reduce staff/writer burden, and what would make
  them trust a new platform?
- Respect platform culture. Tumblr asks, forum comments, Discord community
  channels, and email all have different norms.
- Keep a record of permission: if someone gives feedback, note whether it is
  private, attributed, unattributed, or publishable.
- Do not include private emails, Discord handles, or personal data in repo docs
  unless they are explicitly published as contact channels and still relevant
  to the research purpose.

## Priority Reading And Outreach Leads

### The Angry GM / Scott Rehm

Public surface:

- `theangrygm.com`
- Relevant article:
  <https://theangrygm.com/a-complete-idiots-guide-to-play-by-post-gaming/>

Why this lead matters:

- The article is useful because it is skeptical. It identifies what must change
  for asynchronous play to work rather than assuming PBP is automatically good.
- The strongest product insight is the distinction between low-content,
  high-frequency negotiation and high-content, low-frequency action passes.
- He argues that asynchrony pushes games toward fewer messages that move more
  state, simultaneous action polling, reduced back-and-forth, and systems that
  avoid interrupt-heavy resolution.

What he seems to care about:

- Pacing.
- System fit.
- Dynamic exchange.
- GM workload.
- Whether the medium preserves social energy.

Elbysodic questions:

- Are `needs reply`, `waiting`, `ready for scene`, and backstage states enough
  to reduce low-value back-and-forth?
- Can Elbysodic design scenes and plotting rooms around action passes,
  summaries, and intent capture rather than endless negotiation?
- What would make a PBP product tolerable to someone who dislikes PBP?

Outreach fit:

- High-signal critique candidate, especially for TTRPG/PbP pacing and system
  design. Treat as a critical reader, not a guaranteed enthusiast.

## Research Themes From These Leads

### Pacing Is Product Design

Repeated sources treat pacing as the central PBP problem. The proposed
solutions cluster around:

- posting frequency expectations
- GM/director cadence
- simultaneous action passes
- full-turn posts
- proactive rolls or if/then intent
- summaries and state blocks
- side initiative
- montages and fast-forwards
- graceful skip/NPC/side-line policies

Elbysodic implication:

- `needs reply`, `waiting`, `caught up`, `ready for scene`, and "director
  needed" should be backed by product mechanics, not just badges.

### Commitment Needs Off-Ramps

Sources repeatedly mention abandoned games, AFK players, ghosting, and fading
interest. The strongest advice does not shame people; it creates expectations,
short arcs, test adventures, idle policies, and graceful exits.

Elbysodic implication:

- Add humane states for paused, slow, dropped, transferred, archived, waiting,
  and seeking replacement. Do not reduce commitment to punitive activity
  metrics.

### Good Posts Create Handles For Others

Nerdarchy and PBP guides emphasize that posts should give others something to
respond to. Silent reaction, overlong self-performance, and internal-only prose
stall scenes.

Elbysodic implication:

- Composer and scene guidance can gently encourage hooks, spoken dialogue,
  clear actions, and OOC intent notes without policing prose style.

### OOC Is Not Noise

New School Revolution, Dicehaven, Gamers Plane, and forum/Tumblr guides all
need OOC space: planning, content boundaries, questions, rules, praise,
consent repair, unavailable notices, and staff support.

Elbysodic implication:

- Backstage and plotting rooms should be first-class, object-bound OOC
  infrastructure.

### Platform Builders Converge On Character Identity

Gamers Plane, RP Repository, RPHub, Discord bots, and forum roleplay all solve
some version of "post as the right character" or "show the right profile."

Elbysodic implication:

- Active face is not negotiable. It is the unifying primitive.

### Archives And Content Ownership Matter

Forum Roleplay explicitly warns against deleting member content and emphasizes
backups. OngoingWorlds and PBEM/PBP communities exist partly because generic
tools did not preserve or organize story well enough.

Elbysodic implication:

- Export, backups, archive access, and closure behavior are trust features for
  writers and directors.

## Suggested First Outreach Batch

When Elbysodic has a clickable alpha with first-face onboarding, scene reading,
composer, wanted/backstage, and Studio launch room:

1. RPHub / Hanabi: modern RP platform-builder critique.
2. RP Schtuff / Jeanette: Tumblr/indie newcomer and etiquette critique.
3. Forum Roleplay: forum admin and preservation critique.
4. OngoingWorlds / David Ball or Charles Star: PBP platform/history critique.
5. Dicehaven / Stan Shinn: asynchronous commitment and pacing critique.
6. New School Revolution / Yochai Gal: Discord PBP expectations critique.
7. RP Repository community/support: character-profile and privacy critique.

Suggested ask:

```text
We are building Elbysodic, a roleplay-native PBP studio focused on character
identity, scene continuity, wanted/backstage handoffs, applications, claims,
reserves, and director workflows. We are researching what modern roleplay tools
should preserve from forums, Discord, Tumblr, PBP/PBEM, MMO RP, and
character-profile communities.

Would you be open to a 30-minute alpha walkthrough or written critique? We are
especially looking for hard feedback on what would make this useful, what would
make roleplayers distrust it, and what cultural rituals we should not flatten.
```
