# Selected Logo Direction: Circle Terminals

Date: 2026-05-10

Selected candidate: `circle-terminals-primary.svg`

## Why This Direction Works

The mark reads as a compact PBP-native `E` without needing literal forum,
chat, or generic social symbols. Its structure is simple enough for online use:

```text
o ---
o -
o ---
```

The three circles carry face, roster, and public authorship. The bars carry
thread, scene, reply flow, and continuity. Together they imply an `E` without
turning the logo into a plain letterform.

The cyan, magenta, and yellow terminals connect back to Technicolor as
registered color records. The black-key container keeps the mark sharp at small
sizes and gives the product a consistent favicon/app-icon silhouette.

## What Makes It Strong

- Distinctive: `o --- / o - / o ---` is easy to describe, redraw, and remember.
- PBP-native: the symbol can mean roster faces connected to active threads.
- Product-native: it works as a favicon, app icon, topbar mark, and loading
  glyph without needing a mascot or illustration.
- Technicolor-compatible: the color system is structural, not decorative.
- Scalable: the one-color version still works because the silhouette is strong.
- Flexible: the dark container can be used for app contexts, while the bare
  symbol can be tested for larger brand moments.

## Recommended Next Refinement

1. Tune the circle/bar/spine proportions against real browser favicons.
2. Decide whether the primary brand mark always includes the rounded dark
   container.
3. Draw a custom wordmark so the lockup stops relying on system type.
4. Test in product chrome: topbar, footer, login, loading state, social avatar,
   and generated community previews.
5. Create export rules for SVG, PNG, favicon, monochrome, and mask-icon use.

## Open Choice

Use `circle-terminals-primary.svg` as the clean primary. Keep the previous
arrow and overlap experiments as archived research only; they add meaning but
reduce the immediate small-size read.

## Progression Variant

`circle-terminals-progressive.svg` tests:

```text
o --
o -
o ---
```

This may be more powerful if the brand wants a sense of thread growth,
momentum, or continuity building downward. The tradeoff is that the original
`o --- / o - / o ---` reads more immediately as an `E`, while the progressive
variant reads more like a system of scenes resolving into a longer thread.
